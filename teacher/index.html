Read Harold’s story together

Read slowly. Enjoy the pictures. Pause whenever a child has a question.

Before reading

Explain that this is a family recollection. The pictures include illustrations and edited collages, not verified photographs or original records. Some artwork is AI-generated.

The story mentions a parent’s death, trenches and being wounded. A child may skip those details or choose the “Why we remember” section instead.

While reading

Ask: What did Harold do before the war? How did the sound of his bugle become part of family life?

Listen only if the child wants to. The Last Post links open the Australian War Memorial website; preview them and start at a low volume.

After reading

Talk about why people remember on 11 November and how people can care for one another. Children do not need to share private family stories or take part in a particular religious practice.

Offer a drawing, a quiet moment, or a conversation. There is no quiz and no required feeling.
